Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 August 2026

The short version. KeeperPass has no server and no database. There is no KeeperPass account. Your vault is encrypted inside your browser and stored as a single file in your own Google Drive. We cannot read your passwords, because we never receive them and have nowhere to put them. Three third parties do receive something while you use the app — Google, DuckDuckGo, and Have I Been Pwned — and section 6 sets out exactly what each one gets.

3. What we never receive

There is no KeeperPass server that your data passes through, and no KeeperPass database in which anything about you is recorded. In particular, the following never reach us, in any form, encrypted or otherwise:

  • Your master secret, or anything derived from it.
  • Your vault — its items, names, usernames, passwords, notes, custom fields, tags, TOTP secrets, or attachments.
  • The websites you store credentials for, or the sites you visit while the extension is installed.
  • Any page content from the sites you browse.

This is a structural property, not a promise about our intentions. We have not built the infrastructure that would let us collect these things, and the source code that runs in your browser can be inspected to confirm it.

4. Signing in with Google

KeeperPass has no password of its own and no registration form. You sign in through Google's own OAuth flow, using Google Identity Services. Two consequences follow.

The permissions we request

KeeperPass asks Google for exactly these scopes, and no others:

ScopeWhat it allows
openid, email, profileReads your basic profile — your name, email address, and profile picture — so the app can show who is signed in.
drive.fileAccess limited strictly to files KeeperPass itself creates. It cannot see, list, open, or search any other file in your Drive. This is the narrowest Drive scope Google offers.

Your profile information is fetched from Google directly by your browser and used only within that browser session, to display your name and to open your own vault file. It is not logged, stored, or transmitted to any KeeperPass-operated system, because none exists.

Your Google access token

The access token Google issues is held in your browser's sessionStorage so that a page refresh does not force you to sign in again. It is deliberately never written to localStorage, which means it is discarded when you close the tab. It is never transmitted to us.

What Google itself learns

Signing in requires your browser to load Google's script from accounts.google.com and to call Google's APIs. Google therefore sees your IP address, your device and browser, and the fact that you are using KeeperPass, and it handles that under its own policies. See Google's Privacy Policy.

5. Your vault in your Google Drive

KeeperPass creates one folder in your Drive, named keeperpass, containing one file, vault.json.enc. That file is your entire vault.

Before it is ever written to Drive, the vault is compressed and then encrypted in your browser:

  • Your master secret is put through Argon2id — a memory-hard key derivation function — with a 16-byte random salt, 64 MiB of memory, 3 iterations, and a parallelism of 4. These are the same parameters Bitwarden uses in production.
  • The resulting 256-bit key encrypts the vault with AES-256-GCM, using a fresh 12-byte random nonce for every save, so encrypting the same vault twice never produces the same ciphertext.
  • The derivation parameters travel alongside the ciphertext, so vaults written under older settings stay readable when those settings are strengthened.

Google stores the encrypted bytes and cannot decrypt them. Neither can we. Your master secret never leaves your device, and no copy of it exists anywhere else.

The vault file lives in your Drive, under your Google account. It counts against your Google storage, it is subject to Google's terms, and you can delete it yourself at any time without involving us.

6. Third parties your browser contacts

While the app has no back end of its own, it does make requests from your browser to three external services. This is the complete list.

ServiceWhat is sentWhen
Google
(sign-in and Drive)		 Your OAuth credentials, your IP address, and the encrypted vault file. At sign-in, and on every load and save of your vault.
DuckDuckGo
(website icons)		 The hostname from an item's URL field — for example github.com — so an icon can be fetched for it. The item, its username, and its password are never sent. Whenever an item with a URL field is displayed.
Have I Been Pwned
(breach checking)		 The first five hexadecimal characters of the SHA-1 hash of a password. Never the password, and never the full hash. Automatically after each unlock, and when an item is created, edited, or restored.

About the website icons

To show a recognisable icon next to each entry, KeeperPass asks icons.duckduckgo.com for the icon belonging to that entry's web address. The app cannot reach those sites itself, so the alternative is no icons at all.

We want to be direct about the trade-off: this reveals to DuckDuckGo the hostnames of services you hold accounts with — not which account, not your username, and not your password, but the list of sites itself. Requests carry no identifier of you beyond your IP address, and there is currently no setting to turn this off. If that trade-off is not one you want to make, the honest answer today is to run your own build of the app.

About the breach check

KeeperPass checks your stored passwords against the Have I Been Pwned breach corpus. The check runs automatically each time you unlock your vault, and again whenever you add or change an item — it is not something you switch on.

The query is k-anonymous. Your browser computes the SHA-1 hash of a password locally, sends only its first five characters, and receives back every breached hash sharing that prefix — several hundred of them. The comparison happens on your device. Have I Been Pwned therefore cannot tell which password was being asked about, and never sees the password or its full hash. Results are held in memory only and are never written into your vault file.

8. The browser extension

The extension needs broad permissions to do its job, and you should understand what they mean.

It requests access to all websites and runs a small script on the pages you visit. That script exists to find login forms so that it can fill your credentials into them. It reads the structure of forms on the page — where the username and password fields are — and it communicates only with the extension itself.

The page script makes no network requests whatsoever. It has no ability to transmit anything to us or to anyone else, and page content, form values, and your browsing history are never sent off your device. The only outbound connections the extension makes are to Google, for sign-in and for your vault file.

Removing the extension removes its access immediately. Your vault is unaffected.

This runs on the marketing site only. It does not run on the web app or in the browser extension, and it has no access to anything vault-related: no vault contents, no items, no passwords, no Google account data. See Google's Privacy Policy for how Google handles what it collects.

12. How long anything is kept

  • Your vault is kept in your Google Drive until you delete it. We hold no copy and cannot delete it for you.
  • Browser storage is kept until you clear it, or — for your access token — until you close the tab.
  • Analytics data is retained according to the Google Analytics retention setting on our property.
  • Emails you send us are kept for as long as needed to deal with the matter, and then deleted.

We hold no user database, so there is nothing else to retain or to purge.

13. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access your personal data, to have it corrected or erased, to restrict or object to its processing, and to data portability. You may withdraw consent to analytics at any time.

An honest note on how these apply here: because we operate no user database, we usually hold no personal data about you at all. A request to access or erase your data will most often be answered with confirmation that we hold nothing. Your vault is not ours to produce or delete — it is in your own Drive, and you already have full control of it. To export it, use the export feature in the app; to erase it, delete the keeperpass folder from your Drive and revoke KeeperPass's access in your Google account permissions.

To exercise any right, write to hello@keeperpass.com. If you are unhappy with our response, you have the right to lodge a complaint with your national data protection supervisory authority.

15. Security

If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in KeeperPass, please report it to hello@keeperpass.com rather than disclosing it publicly, and we will respond as quickly as we can.
