How it works

One folder. One file. Nothing else.

No account database, no user table, no company infrastructure between you and your passwords. When you sign in, KeeperPass asks Google or Dropbox for permission to see exactly one thing: a folder it creates for itself.

  1. 01

    Sign in with Google or Dropbox

    With Google, one click grants a single Drive scope — drive.file — which only lets KeeperPass see files it creates itself. With Dropbox, access is limited to KeeperPass's own app folder. Everything else in your storage stays invisible to it.

  2. 02

    We create one folder

    The first time you use KeeperPass, it creates a folder named keeperpass (in Dropbox, under Apps) and one file inside it. That's the entire footprint, for as long as you use it.

  3. 03

    You choose a secret

    Your master secret never touches Google or Dropbox and is never transmitted anywhere. It's used inside your browser to derive an encryption key.

  4. 04

    Every change re-encrypts and re-uploads

    Add a password, edit a note, delete an item — each change re-encrypts the whole vault and overwrites that one file. Your provider stores bytes it cannot read.

  5. 05

    Switch providers whenever you like

    In the web app, Settings → Storage → Move Vault copies your vault between Google Drive and Dropbox. The copy is checked before anything else changes, and the old file is renamed, never deleted.

Under the hood

Locked before it ever leaves your device.

Nothing to breach, because there's nothing to hold. Google or Dropbox can host the file. Neither can read it — nor can anyone else, because there's no third party holding the key.

Argon2id + AES-256-GCM

Your secret is run through Argon2id — a memory-hard key-derivation function built to resist GPU cracking, with the same parameters Bitwarden uses. The key then seals the vault with AES-256-GCM: tamper with a single byte, and decryption fails outright instead of quietly returning garbage.

No company database

Password managers get breached because they're one juicy target holding everyone's vaults at once. KeeperPass has no vaults to lose — each one lives in its owner's own storage.

Revoke it in one click

KeeperPass only ever holds a temporary access token, never your data. Remove its access in your Google or Dropbox settings and it's gone instantly — your vault file stays exactly where you left it.

Real breach checking

Every password is checked against Have I Been Pwned using k-anonymity — only the first 5 characters of a SHA-1 hash ever leave your browser. Your actual password never does.

No tracking in the app

The web app and the extension contain no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, and no advertising code. See exactly what moves where in the privacy policy.

Questions

Straight answers.

Still curious? Write to contact@keeperpass.com.

Can KeeperPass read my passwords?

No. Your vault is encrypted in your browser before it is uploaded, with a key derived from a secret that never leaves your device. We never receive the vault, and Google or Dropbox only ever stores ciphertext.

Can it see the other files in my Drive or Dropbox?

No. On Google Drive it uses the drive.file scope, which only covers files KeeperPass creates. On Dropbox it is limited to its own app folder, Apps/keeperpass.

What happens if I forget my secret?

Nobody can recover it — not us, not Google, not Dropbox. "Forgot your secret?" on the unlock screen starts a fresh, empty vault and keeps the old file renamed in your storage, in case the secret ever comes back to you. Use the export feature to keep your own backups.

Can I switch from Google Drive to Dropbox?

Yes, in either direction. In the web app, open Settings → Storage → Move Vault. The vault is copied and checked first; only then is the old file renamed, never deleted.

Does Dropbox work in the extension and mobile app?

Not yet. Dropbox is available in the web app today; the browser extension and the mobile app currently sign in with Google Drive.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is no subscription, no paid tier, and no payment of any kind. The source code is public under the GNU AGPL-3.0.
